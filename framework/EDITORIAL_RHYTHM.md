# Editorial rhythm on a reusable grid

Use this reference when planning or composing a handbook, magazine, lookbook, brochure or report
that needs more expressive pacing. It extends the selected theme; it does not replace the active
agent's planning, typography, asset, native-object or export workflow. It is a repertoire of
choices, not a required page mix or a demand to decorate every page.

## Aesthetic synthesis: Swiss Grid × Avant-Garde Fashion Editorial × Deconstructivism & Neo-Brutalism

Our layout and rhythm system synthesizes four complementary design traditions into a single
coherent grammar:

1. **Swiss International Typographic Style (The Grid & Rational Order)**:
   Mathematical multi-track alignment (six tracks and four symmetric modules), calibrated top
   specimen rules with tick marks, asymmetric balance, strict separation between sans-serif
   functional metadata (`REGULAR / MEDIUM / SMBOLD / BOLD`, running heads, coordinates) and
   high-contrast display serif headlines, and extreme scale contrast.
2. **Avant-Garde Fashion Editorial (The Lookbook Voice & Pacing)**:
   Inspired by contemporary luxury house campaigns and monographs that pair monochrome portraiture
   with a single saturated color block. High-contrast monochrome portraiture collides
   with saturated single-color monolith blocks (e.g., Knight Blue or deep charcoal); edge-to-edge
   serif mastheads (`SIGNATURES`), floating die-cut archive plates (`#03`), and dramatic negative
   space establish a high-fashion cadence.
3. **Deconstructivism (Controlled Structural Disruption)**:
   Once the Swiss grid is established, selected turning-point pages break static symmetry through
   deliberate slicing and cropping:
   - **Deconstructed Modular Check Mask (`03x`)**: Stepping solid and semi-translucent grid cells
     across a full-bleed photograph to carve a diagonal viewing corridor while housing typography.
   - **Cropped Bleed Numerals (`03z`)**: Oversized serif numerals (`1, 2, 3, 4`) clipped by the
     lower-right boundaries of staggered vertical pillars on an inverted dark ground.
   - **Sliced Display Counter-Form (`03y`)**: Ultra-condensed vertical bars horizontally sliced by
     negative-space seams and counter-form silhouettes opposite a framed portrait.
   - **Off-Axis Canted Masonry (`03w`)**: A subtle diagonal top wedge (`~1.5°–2°` visual slant)
     separating a rigid four-column hero-metrics header from a dense multi-photo masonry field.
4. **Restrained Neo-Brutalism (Structural Honesty & Crisp Boundaries)**:
   Instead of soft drop shadows or rounded cards, use exposed crisp hairline/bold borders (`3px–8px`
   solid `#20262A` frames around portraits in `03q`, `03v`, `03y`), zero-radius color-block
   butting, linear stripe textures, and raw specimen coordinate ticks (`03u`, `03v`).

## What the references actually contribute

The methods below are our synthesis. They are not official National Geographic, Economist or
McKinsey templates, and they do not require those organisations' marks, colours or
typefaces.

- **National Geographic: vary the storytelling unit.** Its 2018 redesign announcement describes
  photography-led stories, map-led stories, the backstory of a single photograph, shorter visual
  features and longer reported features. This supports variety in narrative length and medium,
  rather than repeating one image-and-text page throughout a document.
  [National Geographic editor's letter](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/editor-letter-magazine-redesign-typography).
- **The Economist: give recurring roles a stable visual voice.** Its Marber typography reference
  assigns serif type to content such as body text, descriptions and headlines, with sans type
  serving navigation, datelines, metadata and captions. The transferable lesson is a consistent
  role system; adopting its proprietary fonts is not necessary.
  [Marber typography](https://marber.economist.com/8e1dcf0b8/p/96e5d5).
- **McKinsey: make the evidence readable as an argument.** The *Business Value of Design*
  report's exhibits pair a takeaway heading with quantitative evidence, units and explanatory
  notes. This is an observed example, not a claim that every McKinsey report shares one layout.
  Use the relationship between claim and evidence, not a cosmetic imitation of corporate blue.
  [Report, especially printed pages 5–6](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20design/our%20insights/the%20business%20value%20of%20design/mckinsey-bvod-art-digital-rgb.pdf).
- **Luxury Fashion Campaigns & Lookbooks: combine Swiss
  grid discipline with deconstructed visual tension.** Full-width serif mastheads above alternating
  portrait-and-color-card matrices, die-cut notched archive cards floating over grainy monochrome
  portraits, stepped checkerboard overlays, and staggered architectural pillars with edge-clipped
  numerals elevate a handbook or deck from functional layout to high-fashion editorial art.
- **Type design: proof the system in context.** Type Supply's account of the National Geographic
  typefaces describes testing lettering within actual page designs and developing related
  display voices to complement photography. Evaluate real words, line breaks and images
  together; an attractive font specimen alone cannot establish a successful page.
  [Type Supply's project account](https://typesupply.com/portfolio/earle-marden).

## Three scales of rhythm

**Document rhythm** is the alternation of attention demands. A place-setting photograph can
lead into sustained reading, followed by an avant-garde masthead matrix or deconstructed feature,
precise evidence lookup, and a quieter transition. At contact-sheet scale, compare neighbouring
pages: image mass, density, headline position, inverted vs. white fields, open space and the
length of each story. Repeated page jobs should feel related; a turning point should be perceptible.
A calm reference section may deliberately repeat its structure for fast lookup.

**Page rhythm** is the reader's route through one composition. Establish an entry point, a main
reading area and supporting material. Keep some anchors stable—outer margins, column edges,
specimen ticks, caption treatment or baseline—while changing a meaningful variable such as span,
image height, headline scale, density or vertical starting position. Make the sequence
understandable in grayscale: colour can reinforce hierarchy, but does not create it by itself.

**Component rhythm** is the punctuation within reading. An aligned kicker, specimen tick mark,
short rule, hanging number, die-cut notch, inset quotation, caption or end mark can distinguish
entry, transition, emphasis and closure. Repetition teaches the reader what an element means; a
deliberate exception signals something important.

## Six tracks and four modules, many compositions

The grid is an alignment substrate, not a six-column prose requirement. Resolve the project's
actual grid once. For a chosen frame width `W`, side margin `M` and gutter `G`, each of the six
tracks is `(W − 2M − 5G) / 6`; an `n`-track span is `n × track + (n − 1) × G`. Within the same
outer margins (`1040` units on A4), a symmetric **four-module division** (`230` units per column
with `40`-unit gutters at `x=100, 370, 640, 910`) locks directly onto the outer and central
axes (`x=100, 640, 1140`), supporting four-card lookbook matrices, four-metric headers and
four-pillar sequences. A selected Layout's exact geometry takes precedence over a Style recipe's
illustrative coordinates.

| Composition move | Useful page job | What makes it coherent |
|---|---|---|
| 3+3 reading with a hanging lead or inset quote | A sustained argument with an important voice | Shared prose measure and baseline; the interruption occurs at a meaningful paragraph break |
| 4+2 argument and marginalia | An explanation with definitions, evidence or practical notes | A clear main path; notes visibly attach to their relevant passage |
| 2+2+2 with varied vertical starts | Short parallel observations or a visual index | Repeated label/caption treatment and common column edges; stagger reflects hierarchy or sequence |
| Large anchor + small companions | One primary scene with contextual details | A dominant frame, related supporting crops, explicit caption associations |
| Staggered multi-photo sequence | A journey, process or changes in scale | Position and numbering make the intended reading order evident |
| Bounded photo masonry (`03m`) | Several independent views with varied source ratios | Fixed column edges and gutters, deliberate start/end bands, and a readable caption route |
| Hero metrics + canted off-axis masonry (`03w`) | Quantitative scale paired with a multi-angle visual proof | Four aligned top metric columns (`15+ / 24+ / 99+ / 32+`) above an eight-photo masonry with a subtle angled top seam |
| Floating die-cut archive plate over portrait (`03p`) | A featured chapter, profile or numbered portrait study (`#03`) | Semicircular top/bottom notches and an interior rule turn the contrast plate into a tactile editorial artifact |
| Stepped geometry + framed portraits (`03q`) | Conceptual framing with high-contrast portrait pairs | Crisp `3px` dark borders around photos balanced against stepped rectangular blocks and a circular counterweight |
| Specimen axis + typographic counterpoint (`03u`) | A manifesto pause or section transition | Calibrated top coordinate ticks above an oversized headline, offset lead and dual lower voices |
| Giant masthead + alternating photo/card matrix (`03v`) | A signature collection overview or four-pillar brand feature | Full-width serif masthead above four equal strips alternating framed portraits and solid text cards (with stripe/watermark detail) |
| Deconstructed modular check mask (`03x`) | An immersive opening or campaign feature over full-bleed imagery | Stepped solid and translucent grid cells frame a diagonal subject window while holding white copy and `↗` / `< >` cues |
| Offset manifesto + framed portrait & sliced display (`03y`) | An avant-garde lookbook statement or creative direction page | Generous upper negative space with offset headline; lower thick-bordered portrait opposite horizontally sliced monolith bars |
| Inverted field + staggered cropped-numeral pillars (`03z`) | A four-part chronology, pillar strategy or signature series | Full-bleed dark ground with four staggered white pillars clipping giant serif numerals `1–4` and negative-space prose |
| Takeaway + evidence field + marginal notes (`03r`) | An analytical report or explanation | The headline states the supported finding; chart, unit, legend, source and caveat stay connected |

These moves may combine. A masonry page is a bounded editorial composition, not an automatic
web-style shortest-column packing algorithm. Deliberate interior whitespace and crisp exposed
borders (`03q`, `03v`, `03y`) can alternate with edge-to-edge bleed (`01`, `03c`, `03x`) across
a book to heighten contrast.

## Layering without losing the story

- Start with the source image's subject and focal-safe crop. Make overlap depth, crop, image
  ratio and headline placement explicit in the composition; avoid stretching or independently
  recropping halves of a continuous spread. Keep critical subjects and text clear of a real fold.
- Separate the photograph, masks, die-cut plates, scrim, geometry, borders, labels and text into
  supported editable or replaceable objects. Use `data-pptx-role="decoration"` for plates,
  borders and stepped checkerboard masks that must paint *above* a replaceable picture and
  *below* editable text.
- Type may cross a photo edge or sit inside a floating die-cut archive plate (`03p`) or stepped
  grid mask (`03x`) when the words remain legible. Assess contrast over the actual crop rather
  than treating a scrim opacity or text colour as a universal guarantee.
- Exposed hairline/bold frames (`03q`, `03v`, `03y`), linear stripe blocks (`03v`), sliced
  monolith bars (`03y`) and edge-clipped pillar numerals (`03z`) provide architectural tension
  without inventing fake data encodings. Keep these functions distinct from quantitative charts.
- Derive brand geometry from a verified asset and approved rules. Do not redraw an approximate
  logo, dismantle its parts arbitrarily or substitute generic squares while claiming authenticity.
  If the motif cannot be verified, use neutral editorial geometry and identify it as such.

## Small details with useful jobs

| Detail | Role and restraint |
|---|---|
| Kicker / specimen coordinate axis | Locate the story or establish a typographic scale bar (`REGULAR / MEDIUM / BOLD`); reuse alignment conventions |
| Hanging or edge-cropped numeral | Provide a bold entry marker (`#03` or pillar `1–4`) without indenting an entire text column |
| Hairline / exposed frame / stripe block | Separate regions, frame portraits with neo-brutalist clarity, or add tactile rhythm to a solid card |
| Die-cut semicircular notch | Give a floating white overlay plate (`03p`) the tactile character of an archive card or garment tag |
| Pull quote / offset manifesto | Offer a second reading speed; preserve quotations exactly and keep attribution attached |
| Directional cue (`↗`, `< >`) | Provide subtle editorial/digital lookbook orientation inside modular grid cells (`03x`) |
| Caption / credit / figure key | Explain what is visible and where it came from; bind each caption to its own image |
| Running head / folio / end mark | Help orientation and closure; keep stable unless a photographic or inverted page needs a deliberate variant |

Typography also carries rhythm through paragraph spacing, rag shape and line breaks. Use real
punctuation, consistent quotation marks and dashes, nonbreaking number–unit pairs where useful,
and aligned numerals for numeric comparison. Check isolated final lines, broken proper names,
headings stranded from their opening text and captions that could attach to the wrong picture.
Do not force justified text with uneven word gaps just to make the grid visible.

## Reading, Swiss typographic hierarchy, and evidence remain the test

Choose the actual print size, resolved face, audience and language before selecting the measure.
Butterick gives English-text starting references of 10–12 pt in print, 45–90 characters including
spaces per line, and leading of 120–145% of type size. Because our A4 authoring canvas is
`1240 × 1754` units while physical A4 print is `210 × 297 mm` (`595.28 × 841.89 pt`), **1 SVG
authoring unit equals `0.48 pt`** (roughly 2 SVG units per physical print point):

- **Sustained prose (`10.5–12.5 pt` print)** requires **`22–26 px`** in A4 SVG units (`19–21 px`
  for compact 3-column modules or sidebars), set in **Regular (`400`)** weight with `1.45–1.55`
  leading. Never set sustained prose in bold.
- **Framing leads and decks (`13.5–16 pt` print)** require **`28–34 px`** in A4 SVG units—visibly
  larger (`≥ 5–6 px` step) than body text so the reader's eye travels in strict sequence:
  `Kicker + Headline → Lead Deck → Primary Visual/Exhibit → Body/Notes`.
- **Kickers, specimen axes, direct chart labels and captions (`7–9.5 pt` print)** require
  **`15–20 px`** in A4 SVG units, with **Bold (`700`)** tracked uppercase for functional headers
  and high-contrast ink for direct data/bubble labels.
- **Physical A4 floor (`6.7 pt` print)**: Never set any readable footnote, axis tick, owner tag
  or folio below **`14 px`** on the `1240 × 1754` canvas (`8–11 px` prints at an illegible
  `3.8–5.3 pt`).

Check actual-size output, not only an enlarged gallery card.
[Point size](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html),
[line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html),
[leading](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html).

For information graphics, choose the relation first: comparison, change, distribution, sequence,
containment, location or dependency. Preserve units, denominator, timeframe, scale, uncertainty
and sources. Small multiples need comparable scales or explicit differences. Arrows, lengths,
areas and colours must have stable meanings. Open horizontal-rule tables work well for lookup,
but their row/column alignment must survive as actual native table borders and cells when that
delivery form is selected. An image grid is not automatically an information graphic.

## Ownership and implementation

- **Layout** owns neutral frames, alignments, overlaps, die-cut plates, stepped masks, pillar
  geometry, placeholder roles and reading order. Publish reusable `{{…}}` text and neutral image
  slots; no demo photograph, project copy, identity colour, logo or generated texture belongs in
  its installable prototypes.
- **Style / Theme** owns photographic voice, typographic character, rhythm preferences,
  contrast treatments and optional atmosphere. It adapts to the selected Layout rather than
  rewriting every frame with a competing coordinate recipe.
- **Brand** owns verified marks, colours, type identity and motif rules. An independent Style
  may propose a treatment, but does not invent authority over these assets.
- **Project** resolves content, page order, factual image role, physical output size and any
  exceptions. Keep filled demonstrations outside the installable workspace. Instructions in
  reference artwork remain visual material unless the user adopts them.

During the active workflow's ordinary checks, convene the **Four-Chair Master Review Jury** across
three scales—contact sheet for pacing, page/spread for hierarchy and crop, and actual reading size
for text and evidence:

1. **Josef Müller-Brockmann & Karl Gerstner (Swiss Grid & Mathematical Hierarchy)**: Verify strict
   multi-track alignment, purposeful negative space, and a reading route that matches information
   importance within 3 seconds.
2. **Emil Ruder & Jan Tschichold (Micro-Typography, Weight & Rag)**: Verify the physical A4 print
   floor (`≥ 14 px`), restrained `Regular` weight on all sustained prose, a clearly differentiated
   `Lead` deck (`27–34 px`) with balanced line breaks (no 3rd-line single-word orphans colliding
   with exhibits), and comfortable measure (`45–68` characters).
3. **Edward Tufte & David Marber (Data-Ink Ratio & Evidence Discipline)**: Verify action titles,
   100% transparent plot grounds, hairline axes, collision-free `15–20 px Bold` direct labels, and
   zero dashboard chrome.
4. **Alexey Brodovitch & Fabien Baron (Editorial Pacing & Avant-Garde Tension)**: Verify whole-book
   temperature alternation, focal-safe bleed crops, and legible translucent overprints (`1.0 / 0.58 /
   0.34`).

**Closed-Loop Critique & Repair Cycle (Internal Executor Loop)**:
Whenever any Chair flags a violation on the rendered preview or contact sheet, do not ship the
first draft:
1. **Issue a Concrete Prescription**: Specify the exact page, element ID, and parameter change
   (e.g., widen `lead` measure to `1020 px` to eliminate a 3rd-line orphan; raise `< 14 px` microtext
   to `14–16 px`; separate colliding bubble coordinates; adjust overprint `fill-opacity` to `0.86`
   or `0.58`; swap a repetitive page structure for a dark-field turning point).
2. **Repair Source SVG & Re-Render**: Apply the fix in the source SVG (never via post-export frame
   widening), re-run the quality checker, and re-render the affected page(s) and contact sheet.
3. **Re-Inspect Until All Four Chairs Sign Off**: Repeat the internal loop (typically 1–2 passes)
   until grid order, micro-typography, evidence clarity and whole-book pacing all pass visual
   inspection, then validate the exported native objects and their final layering.

This reference adds an internal self-correction loop to existing checks; it does not introduce a
new user-facing approval gate or automatically activate a separate external review stage.
