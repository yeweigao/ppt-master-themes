# Fashion Editorial — Style Specification

> Method and design defaults only. No project communication contract, brand identity, page structure, or SVG prototypes.

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style Name | Fashion Editorial |
| Best Fit | Fashion campaign books, lookbooks, anniversary or archive editions, casting and portrait series, design/culture programmes, art-direction-led proposals where image and typography carry the argument |
| Reusable Intent | Make a small set of photographs and words feel like a designed fashion publication: images change state, cobalt interrupts the grid, type becomes image, and every page still reads clearly |
| Sources | ppt-master-themes theme `fashion`; Swiss International Typographic Style; avant-garde fashion editorial composition; OFL typefaces (Bodoni Moda, Archivo, Six Caps) |

## II. Communication Method

- **Preferred Mode**: custom
- **Mode References**: showcase
- **Mode Behavior**: Showcase pacing for a read-close fashion document: image-led statements alternate with measured reading pages; one governing message per page; choose the narrative order for the task unless source order is required.
- **Argument Flow**: Establish attitude with a full-bleed portrait, make a word or number interrupt it, then open the method through a manifesto/index, a cast or signature sequence, a measured fact page, an object study and a closing statement. The order may change; the alternation between image temperature and reading temperature should not collapse into one layout repeated.
- **Page Message Discipline**: One governing message per page. A fashion statement page may run on a single word, number or short sentence; reading pages carry the complete argument, captions and qualifiers. Microtext is furniture, not a substitute for meaning.
- **Claim Discipline**: Preserve names, dates, quantities and quotations under the project's editing policy. Generic fashion language may be written for the page; collection facts, casting numbers and archive claims must come from the brief. Label generated or illustrative imagery as such, and never present a stock portrait as a named person or a documented event.

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- **Argument Trace**: Fashion language and editorial titles may be authored for the page; material facts, names, numbers and archive claims remain traceable to the brief or a verified source. Distinguish a stylistic cast count from a verified census.
- **Charts**: Let the information model choose native charts for comparison, change or distribution. Recolour them to cobalt, ink and greys; keep labels and values editable. Never place a chart over a busy portrait, and never let a decorative overlap imply a quantity.
- **Tables**: Default to open horizontal-rule tables on paper or ink: bold uppercase headers, a clear top/header rule, lighter row rules, and column alignment instead of vertical lines or enclosing boxes. Preserve complete records and native cells.
- **Information Selection**: Choose the expression from the reader's question. A single hero numeral, a four-column statistic row, a sequence of look panels and a native table each answer different questions; none is a universal page recipe.
- **Sources**: Record image provenance and licence in the project; keep credits and any supplied logos or QR codes accurate and usable.
- **Native Editability**: Keep reading copy, labels, captions, table cells and chart data as native editable objects. Type-as-image is the deliberate exception: it is a generated transparent PNG and should be described as an image treatment, with its source photo and generation command recorded.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Preferred Visual Style**: custom
- **Visual Style References**: photo-editorial, swiss-minimal, zine
- **Visual Style Behavior**: Black-and-white photography changes state across the document: full bleed, framed inset, colour-tinted, and type-mask. Cobalt is an interruption laid over the grid at partial transparency, not a flat background. Oversized Didone words and ultra-condensed capitals carry the editorial voice; hairline rules, specimen ticks, ghost words and rings carry Swiss structure. Deliberate overlap is welcome where the reading order stays obvious; sustained prose keeps a calm field.
- **Composition**: A 12-column grid with 88 px outer margins, 74 px columns and 16 px gutters on a 1240 × 1754 canvas (columns at 88, 178, 268, 358, 448, 538, 628, 718, 808, 898, 988, 1078). Text aligns to column edges; images may bleed to trim. Asymmetry is structural, not accidental: put the weight on one side and leave a quiet counter-field. Giant words may cross a seam, an image edge or a translucent block, but must not collide with microtext. Keep a consistent masthead/footer axis when the document is a series.
- **Reading Measure and Grid**: Resolve actual output size, language and face before fixing prose measure. On A4 (1240 × 1754 authoring units, where 1 unit ≈ 0.48 pt at 210 × 297 mm print), body copy at 23–26 units is 11.0–12.5 pt (Regular weight), leads at 28–32 units are 13.4–15.4 pt, and microtext/captions must stay at 14–16 units (6.7–7.7 pt; never < 14 units); keep English prose near 45–75 characters per line and use the grid for alignment, not as a reason to make six narrow prose columns. Reading blocks usually span four to six columns; statements may run wider. Captions and credits sit on a shared baseline below or beside the image, never floating without an anchor.
- **Density**: Image-led statement pages are sparse and confident; manifesto and reading pages are comfortable; contact sheets and indexes may be dense but keep a clear caption rhythm and open gaps. Do not fill every grid cell.
- **Decoration**: Hairlines, translucent cobalt blocks, rings, ghost words, oversized numerals, specimen ticks and framed crops. Avoid drop shadows, bevelled cards, glossy gradients, emoji and decorative icons that have no informational job. Functional marks such as arrows, plus signs or crop marks are set as typographic furniture.
- **Editorial Rhythm**: Alternate four temperatures: full-bleed image, paper reading page, ink field, cobalt field. Never repeat the same layout for more than two consecutive pages. Change the image state, type scale or ground before changing the grid. Keep recurring anchors (masthead, folio, caption baseline) so the disruption reads as designed rather than inconsistent.
- **Color Behavior**: Cobalt `#1710CC` carries interruption, structure and emphasis; ink `#0A0A0A` carries dark grounds and display type; paper `#F6F4EF` carries reading; white is the only text colour on photographs. Cobalt blocks on photographs use the alpha ladder 0.14 / 0.18 / 0.22 / 0.28 / 0.32 / 0.38 / 0.45 / 0.55 / 0.62 / 0.72 / 0.86 / 0.92. Solid cobalt is reserved for short, absolute statements. Grey `#77736B` and hairline `#D9D5CB` carry secondary text and rules.
- **Typography Character**: High-contrast Didone display with tight tracking; ultra-condensed uppercase capitals for kickers, vertical labels and type-as-image; a neutral grotesque for body, captions and microtext. Uppercase microtext is tracked 0.2–0.26 em. No fourth display family. Numerals are a design element: use tabular alignment in indexes and statistics.
- **Theme DNA**: Hold four things constant on every page. (1) Ground set: paper for reading, ink fields for display, cobalt as an interruption at partial transparency rather than a flat background. (2) Two voices: high-contrast Didone display with ultra-condensed capitals for the editorial voice; a neutral grotesque for body, captions and microtext; never a fourth display family. (3) One structural motif: black-and-white images that change state (full bleed, framed inset, tint, type-mask) over Swiss specimen structure (hairlines, ticks, ghost words, rings). (4) Pacing: the four temperatures — full-bleed image, paper reading page, ink field, cobalt field.
- **Extension Rule**: Examples and prototypes calibrate this Style; they are not a page whitelist. For a page no example shows, keep the Theme DNA, the margins and grid, and the calibrated type ladder fixed, and vary only the declared variables: span, scale, image state, density and ground temperature. A new composition is valid when it sits on the same grid and would pass unremarked between the existing examples.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Preferred Image Rendering**: custom
- **Image Rendering References**: editorial, minimalist-swiss
- **Image Rendering Behavior**: Monochrome, high-contrast portraiture, fabric and street details. The image is not decoration: choose it for gaze, silhouette, negative space and how it will behave under a translucent block or a letterform mask. Prefer natural skin and cloth texture over beauty retouching.
- **Image Usage**: Use supplied images, free-licence stock (Unsplash/Pexels or equivalent) or photographic AI illustrations when the task allows. Do not use scanned magazine editorials, campaign images or images with identifiable brand marks. Record provenance and licence. Use a cast of different faces and body types; do not imply that stock portraits are named models or a real documented cast.
- **Image Treatment**: Convert to monochrome and keep a full tonal range. Four states: (1) full bleed with a subject-safe crop; (2) framed inset with a 4–8 px ink border and paper margin; (3) colour-tinted with a cobalt or ink veil at 0.14–0.32, retaining facial detail; (4) type-mask, where the photograph is composited inside ultra-condensed letterforms as a transparent PNG. Keep critical faces clear of the gutter, text and translucent seams. Do not apply a uniform tint to every image; alternate states deliberately.
- **Icon Treatment**: Almost none. Use rules, numerals, crop marks, arrows and plus signs as typographic furniture rather than an icon set. A functional icon is allowed when it carries a real action or status; keep its stroke weight consistent with the hairlines.

## VII. Review Focus

- Type-as-image is genuinely visible: the photograph must read inside the letterforms at actual size, not collapse into a dark or empty word. Confirm the generated PNG and its source photo.
- Translucent cobalt blocks reveal the photograph or type beneath them; the deck has not exported as flat opaque rectangles. Check the actual file, not only the authoring preview.
- Giant words, numerals and rings do not collide with microtext, captions or the masthead; no text is clipped at a frame edge.
- Reading copy is legible on its actual ground; text over photographs has a deliberate contrast field. Opacity values alone do not prove contrast.
- Image states alternate with purpose; the document does not repeat one layout for more than two consecutive pages.
- Black-and-white imagery retains detail in shadows and highlights; cobalt tinting does not flatten faces.
- Reading text, captions and data remain native and editable; type-as-image is the only rasterised type and is documented as such.
- Fonts are installed or a deliberate fallback is in place; the delivered PPTX names Bodoni Moda, Archivo and Six Caps correctly.
- Every image has recorded provenance and a compatible licence; no magazine scans, campaign images or identifiable brand marks.
- The 12-column grid, caption baselines and folios stay consistent across the series; asymmetry reads as structure, not misalignment.
- Pages without a matching example still read as the same publication: the Theme DNA holds, and only span, scale, image state, density or ground temperature changed.
