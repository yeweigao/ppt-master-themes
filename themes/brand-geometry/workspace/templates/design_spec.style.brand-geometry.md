# Brand Geometry — Style Specification

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style Name | Brand Geometry |
| Best Fit | Branded handbooks, programme brochures, reports and presentations whose identity has useful geometric structure |
| Reusable Intent | Make the visual language recognizably related to the identity while keeping content comfortable to read |
| Sources | The supplied identity's observable geometry; editorial grids and repeated line systems |

## II. Communication Method

- **Preferred Mode**: custom
- **Mode References**: briefing
- **Mode Behavior**: Lead with a clear message, explain through measured modules and evidence, and use a recurring geometric vocabulary to orient the reader. Choose sequence and density for the actual communication task.
- **Argument Flow**: Let the content establish the argument. Recurring shapes can mark sections or connect related page roles, without determining a fixed story sequence.
- **Page Message Discipline**: One governing message per page; decoration sits around an intelligible hierarchy of headline, explanation, evidence and action.
- **Claim Discipline**: Preserve facts and qualifiers under the project's editing policy. Describe observed shapes rather than inventing symbolic meanings for an identity.

## III. Page Role Vocabulary

| Role | Communication Job | Evidence Obligation | Composition Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening / divider | Introduce a topic and establish recognition | Accurate name and relevant orientation | Large derived fragment or interlocking outline, generous title space |
| Reading page | Explain a topic comfortably | Complete relevant argument and qualifiers | Calm prose field; derived fragments at an edge or between zones |
| Visual explainer | Make a relationship easier to understand | Source-supported direction, membership or comparison | Native labels and relationships; brand angles and line weights coordinate the appearance |
| Data / lookup | Support comparison or exact lookup | Values, units, categories and qualifiers | Clear charts or open horizontal-rule tables, with sparse recurring accents |
| Photo feature | Establish real context | Accurate provenance and caption | Trim-edge image and a restrained derived corner or separate contrast field |
| Closing | Make the next action clear | Accurate contact or action route | Echo of the opening motif around a concise action |

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- **Argument Trace**: Derive the decorative vocabulary from visible contours, angles, spacing, repetition and overlap. Record the actual source and color evidence in project planning, outside this reusable method. Material content claims stay traceable to supplied or verified sources.
- Keep official identity artwork complete, proportionate and separate from derived decoration. A fragment or recombination is an editorial motif, not a replacement official mark.
- **Recognition Before Derivation**: Check how strokes connect, cross and terminate, which contours overlap, and how gaps and proportions relate. Naming basic primitives is insufficient. Develop a fragment only when these relationships survive and the result still feels related to the source at page scale. If a proposed motif is merely arbitrary geometry, use quieter independent decoration or the complete supplied artwork instead. A logo-like reconstruction needs accurate source geometry; it must not masquerade as the official mark.
- **Charts**: Let information determine chart and diagram semantics. Brand motifs may coordinate visual treatment, but interlocking ornament does not imply a factual link, and repetition does not imply a quantity. Preserve values, units, scales and qualifiers.
- **Native Editability**: Keep labels, relationships, chart values and table records editable through the selected runtime. Distinguish decorative line bundles from semantic connectors by placement and treatment.
- **Tables**: Prefer open horizontal-rule tables when appropriate: aligned columns, bold headers and restrained row rules. Preserve complete records. Identity-specific geometry stays outside the cell grid.
- **Sources**: Retain actual identity and image provenance in project resources. A generated illustration is not documentary evidence; supplied identity artwork remains a distinct original asset.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Preferred Visual Style**: custom
- **Visual Style Behavior**: Extract a small family of recognizable primitives from the supplied identity. Preserve a common angle, stroke character, interval and overlap logic; vary scale, density and edge placement by page job. Optional soft gradients echo resolved identity colors behind that family. A calm reading field remains dominant on text pages.
- **Composition**: Use an editorial grid; align content precisely while a large edge fragment, line rhythm or overlapping frame establishes the page field. Choose a dominant geometric move rather than adding unrelated ornaments. A motif can recur as a section marker, small accent or large background fragment without copying the full page.
- **Reading Measure**: Resolve physical output size, language, face and reading distance before choosing columns. For A4 close reading, 10–12 pt and roughly 45–75 English characters per line are useful references; a six-track alignment grid can support two columns, main text/sidebar or short modules. Adjust to the actual task.
- **Density**: Opening pages may carry bold geometry; reading and data pages use quieter or smaller fragments clear of prose, chart marks and table cells.
- **Decoration**: Shapes inherit meaningful proportions and repetition from the identity. Open frames, parallel lines, partial contours and intersections are starting options, not required ingredients. Retain the source's character rather than substituting a generic collection of circles and triangles.
- **Color Behavior**: Resolve the actual palette from current identity evidence and confirmed project values. Keep type contrast independent of a gradient. Use tints and alpha to let large geometry recede, with sparse solid accents for recognition. Flat white, colored fields and soft gradients can coexist as page-role variants.
- **Typography Character**: Coordinate with the resolved identity and delivery target. Clear text hierarchy and comfortable prose take priority over matching a mark's geometric appearance. Serif editorial titles and neutral sans body are one compatible option.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Preferred Image Rendering**: corporate-photo
- **Image Usage**: Use suitable supplied, sourced or illustrative generated images for content jobs. A geometry-only page may need no photograph or generated bitmap.
- **Image Treatment**: Prefer subject-safe trim-edge crops when photography is used; maintain complete, undistorted functional marks. Keep image, wash, native ornament and text as separate layers where supported.
- **Icon Treatment**: Use a coherent family only when it helps identification. Derived brand fragments serve identity and rhythm; do not present them as semantic icons without clear labels.

## VII. Review Focus
<!-- visual-review-trigger: explicit-user-only -->

- The recurring vocabulary is traceable to observable identity geometry without replacing or distorting official artwork.
- Scale, spacing, angle and stroke character feel coherent; variations serve distinct page jobs.
- Body text and data remain comfortable to read at delivery size. Gradients and intersections do not obscure them.
- Decorative repetition and overlap do not create false quantities or relationships.
- Derived shapes, gradient fields and authoritative text/data retain appropriate native editability.
